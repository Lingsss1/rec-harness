We present the KDD Cup 2026 Tencent Advertising Algorithm ChallengeTowards Unifying Sequence Modeling
and Feature Interaction for Large-scale Recommendation, a large-scale competition and benchmark for unified
recommendation modeling. The competition focuses on post-click conversion rate (pCVR) prediction. For each
user–ad ranking instance, participants observe two heterogeneous sources of information: (i) non-sequential
multi-field features covering user, ad, context, and pre-computed cross representations, and (ii) chronologically
ordered user behavior histories from multiple behavioral domains, where each behavior is accompanied by
heterogeneous side information such as item attributes, behavior/scenario indicators, and timestamps. The
central challenge is to transform these heterogeneous signals into compatible representations and jointly process
them with ahomogeneous, stackable backbone, enablingeffective cross-source interactionsandhardware-
efficient scaling with high GPU utilization. Submitted models predict the conversion probability of the target
ad and are evaluated by AUC under controlled computational resources and strict inference constraints.
The competition consisted of academic and industry tracks conducted over two online rounds. Submissions
were evaluated by AUC on separate test sets under controlled computational resources and astrict no-ensemble
policy. All teams trained and evaluated their models on the organizer-provided AngelML platform using
large-scale Tencent Advertising logs and standardized access to high-performance GPUs.
The challenge attracted 13,913 registered participants forming 5,746 teams from 52 countries and regions and
907 universities, research institutes, and companies. Fifty academic-track teams and twenty industry-track
teams advanced to Round 2. We subsequently received 46 solution papers, of which 23 were accepted for
presentation at the KDD Cup workshop. We release de-identified public samples, detailed feature statistics,
and starter resources at this linka to support further research on unified recommendation modeling.

1 Introduction
Recommender systems are foundational to bothcontent recommendation(e.g., feeds, short videos, news, e-commerce)
andadvertising(e.g., CTR/CVR prediction and auction-based ranking), shaping user experience, platform engagement,
and revenue. At today’s scale, these systems operate under massive traffic and strict latency constraints, making
billions of decisions daily. For example, Weixin and WeChat have over one billion monthly active users globally,
generating massive volumes of personalized recommendation requests in real time [ 42]. Meanwhile, global digital
advertising spending reached approximately $790 billion in 2024 [ 22], underscoring the economic importance of
large-scale advertising systems. Together, these statistics highlight the broad social and economic impact of advances in
recommender and advertising systems.
Over the past two decades, recommendation research has evolved from collaborative filtering and linear predictors

to deep representation learning and large-scale ranking architectures, broadly progressing along two major branches:
(i)feature interaction models, which model dependencies among multi-field categorical and contextual features, and
(ii)sequential models, which capture users’ dynamically evolving interests from their historical behaviors. Feature
interaction modeling has progressively evolved fromshallow explicit interactiontodeep interaction modelingand,
more recently,large-scale model scaling. Early factorization-based methods in the FM family efficiently model
second-order interactions through inner products between field embeddings [36, 20, 30, 41]. Subsequent DNN-based
architectures, ranging from Wide & Deep and DeepFM to NFM, AutoInt, xDeepFM, and DCN-V2, substantially
enhance representation capacity by combining explicit feature crosses with increasingly expressive implicit or high-order
interaction mechanisms [7, 15, 13, 24, 40, 46]. More recently, as industrial recommenders move toward larger model
capacity, scaling-oriented approaches such as Heterogeneous-MoE, Multi-Embedding, DHEN, Wukong, RankMixer,
RankUp, RankElastor, and UniFormer shift the focus from designing individual feature crosses toward increasing
the usable representation and interaction capacity of the overall model, while addressing challenges in representation
dimensionality, model depth, parameter scaling, and hardware efficiency [31, 14, 53, 54, 62, 5, 23, 4].
In parallel, sequential recommendation has evolved from modeling relatively short behavior histories to capturing
increasingly long and heterogeneous user trajectories. Early and short-sequence models, including SASRec and the
DIN family, develop self-attention, target-aware attention, interest evolution, and temporal interaction mechanisms
to extract user interests from recent behavior sequences [ 21, 59, 58, 11, 60]. As industrial platforms accumulate
much longer behavior histories, long- and lifelong-sequence models such as SIM, TWIN/TWIN-V2, LONGER,
DARE, and MUSE increasingly rely on memory, retrieval, sampling, sequence compression, representation decoupling,
and efficient attention mechanisms to extend the modeling horizon while controlling computational and serving
costs [33, 35, 1, 3, 39, 2, 10, 50, 16, 55].
Towards Unified Modeling.Despite the substantial progress of both branches, feature interaction modeling and
sequential recommendation have historically evolved as largely separate architectural components. Traditional behavior-
aware CTR and ranking models commonly follow a modularencode-then-interactparadigm: a dedicated interest or
sequence-modeling module first compresses user behavior histories into one or a few fixed-length representations, which
are subsequently concatenated with non-sequential user, item, and contextual features and processed by downstream
MLP or feature-interaction networks [59, 58, 11, 6, 35, 28, 38, 60]. This separation remains prevalent in more recent
industrial systems, where increasingly powerful sequence encoders are coupled with separately designed cross, MoE,
or heterogeneous-expert interaction backbones and fused at a later stage [12, 51, 31, 29, 63, 26]. While such modular
designs are effective and allow the two components to evolve independently, they often compress behavioral information
before rich cross-source interaction, limiting repeated bidirectional conditioning between individual behaviors and
non-sequential features and making model scaling dependent on coordinating heterogeneous subnetworks. Motivated
by the growing importance of jointly exploiting these heterogeneous signals, recent studies, including InterFormer,
OneTrans, HyFormer, EST, MixFormer, HeMix, TokenFormer, and UniFormer, have explored increasingly integrated
architectures for jointly modeling sequential and non-sequential information [52, 57, 18, 27, 17, 45, 61, 4].
Nevertheless, unified recommendation modeling remains far from settled. Existing approaches differ substantially
in their tokenization strategies, interaction topologies, sequence compression mechanisms, degree of architectural
homogeneity, and scaling strategies. Moreover, their effectiveness is typically demonstrated on different datasets
and under different feature spaces, sequence lengths, computational budgets, and serving constraints, making direct
comparison difficult. Consequently, it remains unclear which architectural principles are broadly effective, how well
different unified designs scale with model depth and capacity, and whether their accuracy gains can be retained under
realistic GPU and inference constraints.
These open questions motivate a common benchmark for systematically studying unified recommendation modeling
under controlled industrial-scale settings. We therefore organized the KDD Cup 2026 Tencent Advertising Algorithm
ChallengeTowards Unifying Sequence Modeling and Feature Interaction for Large-scale Recommendation, providing
a shared task that jointly exposes heterogeneous multi-field features and multi-domain behavioral sequences under
standardized data, computational, and inference constraints. As illustrated in Figure 1, the challenge particularly
encourages participants to explore effective heterogeneous tokenization, homogeneous and stackable unified blocks,
fine-grained interactions between sequential and non-sequential signals, and hardware-efficient model scaling.

2 Challenge Setting
Problem setting.In industrial recommendation and advertising ranking, each prediction instance jointly depends
on two heterogeneous sources of information: (i) richnon-sequential multi-field features, including user, target-
ad, contextual, dense, and pre-computed cross features, and (ii)multi-domain user behavior histories, where each
historical behavior is associated with heterogeneous side information such as item attributes, behavior type, scenario,
and timestamp. Conventional systems commonly process these two sources with specialized feature-interaction and
sequence-modeling modules and combine their representations only after largely separate processing. Although
effective, such modular designs restrict repeated fine-grained interaction between sequential and non-sequential signals
and make it difficult to construct homogeneous, deeply stackable architectures. Accordingly, the challenge encourages
participants to develop unified and scalable models that jointly exploit both sources under realistic computational and
inference constraints.

Unified modeling.The competition does not prescribe a specific tokenization or interaction architecture. Participants
may independently design how heterogeneous fields, behavior histories, and target information are represented,
compressed, and jointly modeled. As illustrated in Figure 1, the challenge particularly encourages architectures that
map these heterogeneous inputs into compatible representations and repeatedly process them throughhomogeneous and
stackable unified blocks. This formulation allows diverse designs—including field tokens, behavior tokens, query or
intent tokens, sequence summaries, and other learned representations—while retaining the central objective of effective
cross-source interaction and hardware-efficient scaling.
2.1 Competition Protocol
Tracks and eligibility.The competition consisted of two parallel tracks with the same prediction task but independent
leaderboards and track-specific inference constraints. TheAcademic Trackwas reserved for teams whose members
were all current students at universities or colleges, with cross-institution teams permitted. TheIndustry Trackwas
open to other eligible participants, including practitioners from research institutes and companies, and adopted a stricter
inference-efficiency requirement. Participation was team-based: each team consisted of1 to 3 members, and each
participant could belong to only one team.
Competition procedure.The competition comprisedtwo online rounds, followed by solution-paper review and
presentation at the KDD Cup workshop. Round 1 was open to all eligible teams and emphasized broad architectural
exploration. The top-50 teams in the Academic Track and top-20 teams in the Industry Track advanced to Round 2,
where participants were evaluated under substantially larger data and computational scales. After Round 2, finalist
teams submitted technical reports describing their solutions, and accepted works were presented at the KDD Cup
workshop.
Submission, evaluation, and fairness.Across both rounds, teams could make at mostthree submissions per
day. Submissions were evaluated server-side on hidden test sets, with AUC serving as the leaderboard metric among
models satisfying the corresponding track-specific inference-time constraints. To emphasize single-model architectural
innovation and ensure fair comparison,model ensembling was prohibited. Throughout both rounds, the organizing
committee conducted multiple rounds ofAI-assisted code review, supplemented by manual verification, to assess
compliance with the competition rules. The review pipeline analyzed submitted code for prohibited ensemble methods
and potential plagiarism, including suspicious structural and semantic similarities across submissions. Several teams
were found to have employed ensemble strategies, most commonly simple or weighted averaging of predictions from
multiple models. These submissions were deemed non-compliant, and the corresponding results were subsequently
invalidated. To the best of our knowledge, TAAC 2026 is the first large-scale public recommendation competition to
report the use of AI-assisted code review, complemented by manual verification, to enforce an explicit no-ensemble
policy. This human–AI review pipeline enabled scalable inspection of a large number of submissions while preserving
human judgment for final compliance decisions, helping maintain a fair comparison of single-model architectural
innovations.

3 Data
The competition data consist of de-identified Tencent Ads interaction logs and are designed to support the central
problem of this challenge: jointly modeling user behavior sequences and non-sequential multi-field features within
a unified backbone. Each prediction instance corresponds to a user–item ranking opportunity with a response label,
a target timestamp, static or slowly changing user/item fields, and multi-domain historical behavior sequences. The
public data resources include both first-round and second-round samples; both use flat Parquet files to expose typed
feature columns for model development.2
Dataset construction.The competition dataset was constructed from attributed pCVR advertising logs from Weixin
Channels. To improve the representativeness and difficulty of the prediction task, we removed all samples associated
with conversion types exhibiting high conversion rates, as well as samples involving dual optimization objectives or
non-optimization objectives.
We adopted a temporally ordered split, with the test window occurring strictly after the training period. Users
were kept disjoint between the training and test sets to prevent user-level information leakage. To reduce temporal
sampling bias, test instances were sampled proportionally across all 24 hours of the day. Likewise, the training set was
proportionally sampled from multiple days, preserving the temporal coverage of the original logs.

4.3 Evaluation Protocol
Evaluation was conducted in a controlled server-side environment on the official test set. Test labels remained
confidential. Participants submitted prediction files or containerized inference code, and the official evaluation service
computed AUC without exposing the labels.
4.4 Competition Platform
We hosted the challenge on a Tencent-operated official competition website and ran all training, validation, and
submission evaluation through our in-houseAngelMLplatform, providing a uniform and reproducible environment
for all teams. The website served as the single source of truth for registration, rules, data documentation, baselines,
announcements, and leaderboard status. Importantly, the platform isaccessible to participants worldwide, ensuring

worldwide. Participants represented universities, research institutes, and major technology companies, reflecting strong

5.2 Solution Overview
We summarize the solutions of the three highest-ranked teams in each track, as well as the two innovation-award-winning
teams. See the corresponding workshop papers for further details.
Academic Track: First Place.The winning team proposed CRAFT, which handles sparse categorical fields, dense
vectors, contextual features, and multi-domain behavior histories through type-aware tokenization and a shared stackable
backbone. Its central design replaces interaction-centric cross-layer propagation with contextual feature transport:
non-sequential features are reliability-weighted to produce sample-specific control signals that regulate how intent and
sequence representations evolve across depth.
8

PAGE 9
For each behavior domain, CRAFT generates intent tokens from non-sequential context and pooled behavioral
evidence. Within every block, the contextual transport module predicts channel-wise scaling, bias, and bounded memory
signals for intent and sequence states, producing a context-conditioned representation displacement while preserving
the initial information path. Sequence encoders then refine behavioral dynamics, intent tokens retrieve sequence
evidence through cross-attention, and a token-subspace mixer performs local field refinement. The final ranking score is
computed from the resulting intent representations rather than directly from the original feature tokens.
This design provides a unified interface between heterogeneous context and behavioral evidence without relying
on late fusion or independent feature and sequence towers. The distilled CRAFT model achieved a test AUC of
0.838090, surpassing the interaction-based distilled baseline at 0.837983. Stacking CRAFT to six blocks further
achieved0.838148, while a laterd model = 480configuration reached a post-submission test AUC of0.838192.
Academic Track: Second Place.The runner-up team proposed the UniDAC architecture for large-scale CVR
prediction. The model is motivated by three characteristics of industrial ranking data: strong non-sequential features can
dominate sequential signals, user behaviors are fragmented across multiple domains, and naive parameter scaling often
brings limited gains. Building on a HyFormer-style query-based framework [18], UniDAC introduces a Dual-stack
Encoding scheme to strengthen sequential modeling. A LONGER branch [2] compresses old histories into a summary
token while preserving recent events to capture long-range interest evolution, whereas a parallel attention-free SwiGLU
branch [37] focuses on local event-level patterns. UniDAC further introduces a Cross-domain Interaction module,
which summarizes each behavior domain into a domain-level token, exchanges complementary information through
cross-domain attention, and injects the resulting context back into each sequence via gated residual connections.
Candidate-aware query generation and RankMixer-style token mixing [62] are used to improve the interaction between
target-conditioned sequential evidence and non-sequential features, while a SoftMoE prediction head [34] expands the
output-side modeling capacity. For efficient scaling, the model controls high-cardinality sparse embeddings through
fixed-budget hashing and ID dropout and adopts FlashAttention-2 [8] to reduce the cost of long-sequence attention.
Together, these scaling-friendly architectural and system optimizations enabled the model to scale from 100M to 1B
parameters, achieving an AUC of 0.83792 and winning second place in the Academic Track.
Academic Track: Third Place.The third-place team proposedUniTokenFormer, a unified token-enhanced frame-
work for post-click conversion rate (pCVR) prediction built on HyFormer [18]. Instead of treating tokenization as a
fixed preprocessing step, the method systematically designs token construction, target-aware enhancement, and capacity
allocation. Heterogeneous user-side and item-side fields are first concatenated and projected before being split into
multiple non-sequential tokens, preserving cross-field interactions as token capacity increases. Candidate-item tokens
further condition multi-domain behavior sequences and generate DIN-style interest tokens [59]. User, item, and interest
tokens are fused before domain-specific queries decode the behavior sequences with LENS-enhanced target-conditioned
gates and positional biases [48], while domain auxiliary objectives strengthen per-sequence supervision. The method
further treats token allocation as an explicit scaling dimension. The study showed that, under comparable dense model
sizes, increasing item-side tokens was more effective than increasing user-side tokens, while an item-heavy joint
allocation enabled a single-block model to scale to 1.063 billion dense parameters. Combined with field-wise feature
preprocessing, bidirectional time-gap encoding, grouped sparse/dense optimization, exponential moving averages, BF16
training, and graph compilation, the approach achieved consistent gains over the official HyFormer baseline.
Industry Track: First Place.The winning team proposedQueryFormer, a unified post-click conversion rate
(pCVR) model for jointly modeling heterogeneous non-sequential fields and multi-domain behavioral sequences.
Building on recent progress in unified modeling [ 27], the model replaces projection-only query construction with
explicit token-to-query attention. Its stackable field–sequence block first refines user and item field tokens with self-
and cross-attention. Item tokens then retrieve evidence from four behavior domains, while behavior-initialized probes
cross-attend to the refined field context to generate queries for subsequent sequence interaction. To scale capacity under
strict inference constraints, QueryFormer organizes tokenization as an embedding matrix with H independent columns.
These views are evaluated in parallel, and their sequence queries are packed into shared-parameter cross-attention to
amortize key/value projections and GPU kernel launches. Within the team’s experimental grid, increasingH consistently
improved validation AUC: the H=4 configuration gained 0.00066 AUC over H=1, while H=8 required only 1.89×
the per-sample inference latency of H=1. Replacing the cross-attention query generator with an MLP caused the largest
9

PAGE 10
individual ablation drop, supporting query generation as the main architectural contribution. QueryFormer achieved
first place in the Industry Track with an official test AUC of 0.83254.
Industry Track: Second Place.The runner-up team proposedUniDot, whose central component is the FM Highway,
a dedicated path that carries explicit dot-product feature interactions around the residual stack and delivers them directly
to the classifier [25]. Rather than computing second-order interactions only once at the input, as in FM [36] and its deep
variants, the model emits a factorization-machine term at every layer from that layer’s current token representations,
so interactions are re-derived as the representations evolve in depth. This places the design alongside a line of recent
industrial recommenders that reintroduce an explicit dot-product matching step inside an otherwise attention-based
backbone [47], but routes those scores past the trunk entirely, so explicit low-order signals are not diluted by repeated
nonlinear mixing and the classifier retains direct access to interaction evidence at every level of abstraction. The
highway allows the rest of the network to form a single homogeneous stack: because explicit interactions are guaranteed
a route to the output, the trunk is free to specialize in implicit mixing. The trunk stacks a repeated macro-block in
which a token-mixing bus and a sequence-retrieval bus (item tokens cross-attending to the behavior histories) operate
in parallel over a shared token space. The design is motivated by the observation that the embedding inner product
of collaborative filtering and the query–key score of dot-product attention [ 44] share the same primitive, while the
two buses exchange state at every layer through an MLP-Mixer [43] fusion. The sequence side is embedded once per
forward pass and shared by all consumers, bounding inference latency [47]. Training uses a dual sparse/dense optimizer
(Adagrad [9] on the embedding tables and Muon [19] on the dense parameters), an auxiliary conversion-delay head,
and multi-path mutual learning [56] across the model’s parallel prediction paths.
Industry Track: Third Place.The third-place team proposedUniBridge, a stackabledecode-then-mixarchitecture
for jointly modeling behavioral sequences and multi-field features. UniBridge first applies lightweight local temporal
modeling to each behavior stream. Queries conditioned on the candidate item then attend to the behavior history and
compress it into a small set of intent tokens. This design reduces the cost of long-sequence modeling and avoids
applying global self-attention to the full behavior history [2]. The resulting behavior tokens are combined with user and
item field tokens in a shared residual stream. In addition, a parameter-free RankMixer rewire [62] is used to model their
interactions. Compared with fully unified Transformer architectures [57], UniBridge keeps the number of mixed tokens
small and fixed. This design supports early interaction between sequence and field features with low computational
overhead.
Winner of the Unified Block Innovation Award.The awarded team proposed MaskRec, a topology-masked unified
architecture for jointly modeling heterogeneous feature interactions and multi-domain user behavior sequences. Building
on recent efforts to unify feature interaction and sequence modeling in industrial recommender systems [18, 57, 61],
MaskRec transforms heterogeneous non-sequential features, contextual signals, multi-domain behavior sequences, and
auxiliary representations into a shared token space and models them with a unified Transformer-style backbone. Its core
component, TopoMask, extends the masking mechanism of Transformer attention [44] from temporal constraints to
topology-aware interaction constraints, explicitly regulating token-level query–key connections according to token roles
and behavior-domain relationships. Global memory tokens and domain-level memory tokens are further introduced as
learnable aggregation nodes for global and domain-specific information. To inject candidate-conditioned interaction
signals before unified modeling, MaskRec further introduces DualQ, which performs bidirectional cross-attention
between user-side and candidate-item-side tokens, followed by asymmetric attentive pooling and domain-conditioned
feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) [ 32] to generate domain-aware query tokens. The resulting design enables
source-local modeling, domain-level aggregation, and controlled cross-source interaction within the same backbone and
consistently outperforms the HyFormer-style baseline on the competition dataset.
Winner of the Scaling Law Innovation Award.The awarded team developed CoSFormer, a single-model architecture
for post-click conversion prediction built on the competition’s HyFormer-style baseline [18]. Its central contribution is
information-bearing token co-scaling. In the RankMixer-style backbone [62], model width factorizes asdmodel =T dsub,
where T is the number of query and context tokens. Rather than widening an unchanged representation, CoSFormer
fixes dsub = 4, appends tokens derived from new predictive signals, and widens the shared backbone in lockstep. The
resulting 48-token stream unifies static context and four behavior sequences through independently projected pretrained
vectors, explicit user–item crosses, TIN-based target-time readouts [60], and multi-resolution history summaries. A

PAGE 11
MaskNet-inspired dual stream [49], token-type-private feed-forward networks, and candidate-aware attention further
process these heterogeneous channels.
To isolate the scaling gain, the team evaluated a nine-step semantic-token ladder against two counterfactuals:
widening the existing bank and appending learned empty tokens at a fixed per-token subspace. Width-only scaling
degraded performance from the first scaling step, while empty tokens produced short-lived gains before turning
downward. Semantic co-scaling instead improved AUC until later token families became redundant, providing an
empirical stopping rule. The results showed that useful scaling came from growing predictive information and model
capacity together, rather than from token count or parameter count alone. Interestingly, this finding independently
aligns with the scaling philosophy of RankUp [5], which also expands model capacity by constructing richer and more
informative input representations rather than simply widening hidden layers or embedding dimensions. Together, these
independent results suggest that effective scaling in recommendation may depend not only on making modelswider,
but also on making the representations being scaledmore informative.
6 Conclusion
This paper presents the KDD Cup 2026 Tencent Advertising Algorithm Challenge, a large-scale benchmark for
jointly modeling heterogeneous multi-field features and multi-domain user behavior sequences under controlled data,
computational, and inference constraints. Through two competition tracks and two progressively scaled rounds, the
challenge provides a common setting for systematically evaluating unified recommendation architectures and studying
their effectiveness, stackability, scalability, and hardware efficiency. The competition results reveal several recurring
design principles. Successful solutions increasingly move beyond conventional late-fusion pipelines by introducing
richer tokenization, target- or intent-aware cross-source interaction, and stackable mechanisms for jointly processing
sequential and non-sequential signals. Meanwhile, the strongest scaling-oriented solutions suggest that increasing
model capacity is most effective when accompanied by correspondingly richer and more informative representations,
rather than by naively increasing hidden dimensions or parameter counts alone.
Together, these findings highlight that the next stage of large-scale recommendation modeling requires the co-design
ofrepresentation, interaction, scaling, and system efficiency. We hope that the released de-identified data samples,
feature statistics, starter resources, winning solutions, and competition findings will provide a useful foundation for
future research on unified and scalable recommender systems.
